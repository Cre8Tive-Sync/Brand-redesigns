    The system behind it.

    One idea runs through everything: IPS works the night so your building is ready by morning. A sky that tells the time, two accents, two typefaces and one way of moving: fast, then done.

    The key fob

    The thing a client actually hands over. A key tag, drawn in one line weight, beside a heavy, wide wordmark. It reads at 16px and on a van door, and nobody else in the category owns it.

    The colour tells the time.

    The palette is the night itself. Pages move from indigo dusk through a violet pre-dawn to an apricot sunrise as you read, so every screen knows what hour it is. Apricot is the crew's work lamp and marks work on the dark; violet carries the energy on the light.

    Loud on top, calm underneath.

    Archivo is a variable grotesk that runs from condensed to wide and thin to black. Headlines, hours and figures are set heavy and condensed, in capitals, and they move: words snap into place and lean with your scroll. Hanken Grotesk does the reading. Two families instead of five.

    Out in the world

    IPS is mostly seen at night, by tenants who never meet the crew. The brand lives on the things they leave behind.

    How it moves

    Motion behaves like a crew on shift: quick, decisive, then still. Nothing bounces.

    Scrub, don't play

    Scenes are tied to scroll, so the visitor sets the pace. Time moves when they move.

    Snap, never bounce

    Words arrive stretched and snap tight on one sharp ease-out. No overshoot, no wobble: they arrive and stay arrived.

    Light means work

    Light appears only where work is happening, then dims to an apricot mark once it's signed off.
